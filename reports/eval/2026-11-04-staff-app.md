# Staff companion: live test, 4 Nov 2026 (00:58–01:35 UTC)

**What was tested:** the staff phone app (`/staff`, branch `p1/staff-app`) against a local API
built from this branch. The local API ran in live mode, with the runtime on, prototype mode on
and actions not in dry run. It used the live models: Muse Spark 1.3, Muse Voice Transcribe and
hosted SAM 3.1 on Meta's API, plus our serverless SAM 3.1 and Muse Glimmer 30B endpoints on
RunPod. The phone was the desktop app's browser pane at 375×812 (mobile emulation). The deployed
API pod was not touched.

**What was fed:** a supermarket checkout stock clip (`data/testclips/market/p8421356.mp4`, 8.4 s:
a cashier hands produce into a customer's red basket). It was uploaded as a paired phone camera
in 3 s chunks through `POST /pair/{token}/chunk`. The first chunk of every upload was refused by
Pair-a-Phone's one-request-per-second limit, because it came right after the claim, so each run
used the clip's last two chunks.

**What was made by hand:** the rules. They were set with `PUT /runtime/plan`; the Planner was not
used. Everything else was a real model call: SAM 3.1 detections, the rule engine, Glimmer's
verdicts, the escalation timers, Spark's spoken lines, Voice Transcribe and the Ask agent.
Nothing is SAMPLE data.

| phone-2 | same | `no_violation 0.12`, `no_violation 0.08` ("a staff member is serving the customer") | Nothing. The verifier suppressed both, correctly |
| phone-3 | "Tell the till lead whenever a customer with a shopping basket is at the checkout, …" | `violation 0.95`, `unsure 0.08` | One verified alert to the till lead. Nobody acked it, so it went to the store manager 46 s later |

| phone-6 | "A customer with a shopping basket is at the checkout." | `no_violation 0.05`, `no_violation 0.08` (says the condition is met, but a fact is not a violation) | Nothing |
| phone-7 | "Staff must wear gloves when handling unpackaged fresh produce at the till." | `violation 0.90`, `violation 0.95` ("handling unpackaged leafy vegetables … with bare hands and no gloves") | **Two verified alerts.** One was acknowledged, put on the way and marked done. The other escalated and was then tapped False alarm |

Glimmer judges whether the *rule* is broken. When the rule describes something to help with
rather than something forbidden, it tends to say `no_violation` or `unsure`. It reasons about
whether help is already being given, or whether the notification happened. On the same rule text
and the same clip it went from `violation 0.95` (phone-3) to `unsure` (phone-4). A rule worded
as an obligation, as Planner-compiled rules are, gave two confident violations. That is the
verifier doing its job, not the staff app; it is recorded here because the staff app only shows
what passes it.

### Alert to phone

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Alert sent (notify step chained) → phone showed it, as the server measured it (the `/seen` POST round trip is included) | 38 ms, 71.5 ms and 92 ms over the three live deliveries; p50 71.5 ms, p95 90 ms (`GET /staff/stats`) |
| The same, as the phone measured it from the alert's timestamp (same machine, same clock) | 5–12 ms |
| Camera → phone, end to end | 128–134 s on every run where serverless SAM timed out (5 of 6 runs); about 8–20 s when it answered (see below) |

**Where the time goes: serverless SAM, not the phone.** In 6 of 7 runs, *camera → candidate* took
122–124 s. The API log shows `sam_fallback_to_hosted: self-hosted SAM server call failed: POST
/segment_image failed: ReadTimeout`: the serverless SAM endpoint (RTX 4090 pool, five workers
`throttled` at the time) did not answer within its 120 s timeout, and hosted SAM 3.1 then did
the work in 2–3 s. In the one run where the serverless worker answered (phone-6), camera →
candidate took 2.2–2.5 s. From then on, Glimmer's verdict takes 5–18 s once warm (120 s when
cold, in run 1), and the alert reaches the phone in under 0.1 s. So a warm stack puts an alert on
the phone about **8–20 s** after the camera saw it.

### Escalation, false alarm, escalate-to-role

- **Unacknowledged alerts escalated on the real timer** three times, 45.7–46.4 s after the alert
  (the 45 s timer plus the 1 s tick). Each went from `till_lead` to `store_manager`, and the
  store manager's feed showed it.
- **Acknowledging stopped the timer.** On `ev-f086…` the countdown disappeared and no store
  manager alert was sent.
- **False alarm** (UI tap on `ev-910b…`) chained an `ack`, a `label` (`false_alarm`, rule
  `gloves-for-fresh-produce`, role `till_lead`) and the staff `action`. The API logged
  `hard_negative_recorded` and wrote `data/rule_ci/regressions/gloves-for-fresh-produce.jsonl`,
  the same path Telegram and the alerts page use. This alert was in fact correct; it was tapped
  only to exercise the path.
- **Escalate to a role** (`to_role: area_manager`) started a new chain to the area manager, and
  the area manager's feed showed the item.

- **Push-to-talk Ask, by API** with a WAV recorded with `say` (the browser pane has no

  "No empty gaps confirmed", which is correct for a till.

### Bugs found and fixed during the test

1. **The Next.js dev server gzipped the event stream** and held events until its buffer filled,
   so the phone showed nothing live. Fixed with `Cache-Control: no-cache, no-transform` on
   `/staff/stream`.
2. **A stream could stay open but silent** after an API restart, so the phone missed a new alert
   until reload. Fixed: the API sends `ping` every 15 s, and the phone reopens a stream that has
   been silent for 40 s.
3. **The notifier forgets alerts on restart.** The feed now rebuilds those cards, and the role
   history of re-escalated items, from the chain's notify and escalation steps. It also finds the
   strip folder by camera and event, because the runtime chains the candidate before it draws the
   strip.

## Web Push with the app closed (added 01:50–01:54 UTC)

**Where push does not work.** The desktop app's browser pane cannot register a service worker,
and headless Chromium refuses push (`AbortError: Registration failed - push service not
available`). Google Chrome is not installed on this Mac, and I did not install it.

**Where it was proved.** Playwright's Chromium 156 (a Chrome for Testing build) in a headed
window. It subscribed through Google's push service; the endpoint is on `jmt17.google.com`.
Script: `push_check.py` in the session scratchpad. It allows notifications, opens `/staff` as
`till_lead`, closes the app page (only the manifest tab stays open), raises an alert, then
reads `registration.getNotifications()` from that other tab.

1. **A drill.** Raised at 01:52:55.19. The relay pushed it, and Google returned `201` at
   01:52:55.91. The service worker showed *"DRILL: Spill at till 2"* with the actions
   `Acknowledge` and `Open`, while the app was closed.
2. **A Glimmer-verified alert** (the gloves rule, checkout clip, camera `phone-8`). Serverless
   SAM was warm this time:

   | Step | Time (UTC) | Since the camera chunk |
   |---|---|---|
   | Camera chunk | 01:53:35.58 | 0 |
   | Candidate chained | 01:53:37.26 | 1.7 s |
   | Glimmer `violation 0.95` (6.3 s call) and the alert | 01:53:43.63 | 8.1 s |
   | Push accepted by Google (`201`) | 01:53:44.48 | 8.9 s |

   The notification read *"Alert: Staff must wear gloves when handling unpackaged fresh produce
   at the till."*, with the body *"muse-glimmer-30b: violation 95% · Till 2 · Camera phone-8 at
   01:53:34 UTC (medium)"*, and the actions `Acknowledge` and `Open`. The test then opened the
   Acknowledge link (`/staff?item=ev-1d18…&ack=1`). The item went to `acknowledged`, and a
   chained `ack` plus a staff `action` followed 2.2 s after the push
   ([screenshot](../../ui/public/screens/staff-push-deeplink.png)).

The test could not click the operating system's notification button, so the Acknowledge path
was tested by opening the same URL the service worker opens.

## Not tested or not done

- Crowd warnings were not raised live; that needs a watched crowd camera. They use the same
  notifier path, which is covered by `tests/staff`.
- No real phone. Web Push was proved in Playwright's Chromium on this Mac (next section), not
  in Google Chrome or on a phone.
- MMS-TTS (see above).
- The timings come from one machine, so phone and server share a clock. On a real phone over the
