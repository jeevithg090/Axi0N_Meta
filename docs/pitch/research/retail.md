# Rulecam market research: supermarkets, convenience stores, QSR and small chains

Researched 4 Nov 2026, in about 40 minutes, for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon (about 16 hours left). Every claim has a URL. "Unverified" means the number comes from a vendor, a law firm, a review aggregator or a secondhand report, and we did not find the original. Prices are rarely published in this market. Most of the dollar figures below are third-party estimates or numbers quoted in news stories.

**Bottom line.** Plain-language camera alerts are no longer new. Brivo Eeva, Coram, Lumana, Hakimo and Lytx all sell some version of them. Rulecam should never call itself first. The gaps that are still open for a small shop are these:

- proof that a hazard was **seen and fixed**, not just detected;
- shelf availability **without new hardware**;
- answers about the footage that come with **numbers and a receipt**;
- food-safety self-checks that do **not** watch individual workers.

Every one of these runs on the modules we already have.

## 2. What owners complain about, with evidence

We found no usable Reddit threads (r/smallbusiness, r/KitchenConfidential and r/grocery came back empty in our searches). The evidence below comes from news, review sites and regulators.

1. **False alarms teach people to ignore alerts.**
   - When cities moved to "verified response", police cited burglar-alarm false-alarm rates of 92% in Los Angeles and 96 to 98% in Milwaukee ([Security Sales](https://www.securitysales.com/news/lapd-still-responding-to-most-alarms-despite-new-policy/), [City of Milwaukee](https://city.milwaukee.gov/police/Information-Services/Burglar-Alarm-Policy)). The data is old, but the pattern holds.
   - Walmart workers complained about Everseen's false positives ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/walmart-employee-complaints-ai)).
   - Rite Aid's face matching produced thousands of false matches ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/19/ftc-bars-rite-aid-from-using-facial-recognition-under-settlement.html)).
   - Even Veesion's own customer story admits to some false alarms ([ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/local-news/investigations/how-ai-is-stopping-shoplifters-including-here-in-the-valley)).
2. **Nobody watches the footage.**
   - One retailer reported that less than 1% of its recorded video is ever viewed ([Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/76977-so-why-isnt-shrink-shrinking-1)).
   - 39% of retailers say finding the right footage after a loss takes too long ([Security Buyer](https://securitybuyer.com/?p=27904)).
   - Small shops often check cameras only after something happens (vendor blog, [Reolink](https://reolink.com/blog/how-often-do-stores-check-cameras)).
3. **Cost, licences and lock-in.**
   - Some camera systems stop working without a licence: every Rhombus camera needs one ([Rhombus pricing guide](https://getsafeandsound.com/blog/rhombus-pricing/)), and one G2 reviewer says the same of Verkada ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/verkada/reviews_and_filters)).
   - Spot AI costs about $99 per camera per month (unverified).
   - Quote-only pricing frustrates buyers ([rfp.wiki on Coram](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/coram-ai)).
   - Vendors can disappear: Grabango closed ([Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/grabango-ceases-operations-automation/729590/)).
4. **New hardware is required.**
   - Verkada's search needs newer Verkada cameras.
   - Focal needs a camera every 8 feet of shelf.
   - Simbe is a robot, Standard AI uses its own ceiling cameras, and Coram needs its NVR box (sources in section 1).
5. **Watching workers causes backlash.**
   - Outback cancelled a pilot after backlash ([CCW](https://customercontactweekdigital.com/tools-technologies/news/outback-steakhouse-nixes-employee-surveillance-program-following-customer-backlash)).
   - Burger King's "Patty" headset, which scores staff friendliness, drew criticism ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/burger-king-ai-assistant-patty-headsets-restaurants/)).
   - Agot was described as spying on workers ([The Takeout](https://thetakeout.com/startup-wrong-food-drive-thru-order-1847695904)).
6. **Owners don't trust vendor claims.**
   - Veesion tried to hire actors to play store owners ([ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/local-news/investigations/how-ai-is-stopping-shoplifters-including-here-in-the-valley)).
   - Just Walk Out was reportedly reviewed by people ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/companies/news/amazon-s-just-walk-out-checkout-tech-was-powered-by-1-000-indian-workers-124040400463_1.html)).
   - Presto's voice ordering reportedly needed people on about 70% of orders ([The Star](https://thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2023/12/08/ai-drive-thru-firm-reveals-humans-needed-on-70-of-orders)).
   - This is why "verify it yourself" is worth a lot.
7. **The theft problem is changing.** NRF's 2026 report found shoplifting incidents fell 12.4% in 2025, while fraud and scams kept rising ([NRF](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/retailers-see-fraud-schemes-evolve-as-shoplifting-declines-nrf-study-finds), [SecurityInfoWatch](https://www.securityinfowatch.com/retail/news/55396090/national-retail-federation-nrf-study-shoplifting-declined-in-2025-as-retail-fraud-schemes-increased)). A pitch built only on "AI stops shoplifters" is weaker than it was in 2023.

### The costs a small shop actually carries

- **Empty shelves.**
  - NielsenIQ data, as cited in a report: 7.4% of retail sales in 2021 were lost to out-of-stocks, about $82B ([A Closer Look](https://a-closer-look.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Report-Out-of-Sight-Out-of-Mind-The-Hidden-Cost-of-Empty-Shelves-and-Broken-Displays_FINAL-sm.pdf)).
  - DHL estimates £2.1bn of UK grocery sales are at risk from stock-outs (2026, [DHL](https://www.dhl.com/gb-en/home/press/press-archive/2026/new-dhl-report-reveals-availability-is-the-biggest-driver-of-customer-loyalty.html)).
  - An older GMA study found 40% of shoppers who meet an out-of-stock put off the purchase or shop elsewhere ([Progressive Grocer](https://progressivegrocer.com/gma-study-reducing-out-stocks-can-boost-retail-sales-3-percent)).
- **Queues.**
  - In a 2026 survey of convenience-store shoppers, about 30% would leave a three-person line and half would leave at four. The survey comes from a checkout vendor ([Mashgin](https://www.mashgin.com/reports/c-store-checkout-preference-report)).
  - NACS is covering the same theme ([NACS, Oct 2026](https://convenience.org/stay-current/news/2026/october/20/cut-the-line-to-keep-customers)).
- **Slips and falls.** These figures are attributed to the National Floor Safety Institute but come secondhand, so they are unverified:
  - about $450M a year in grocery slip-and-fall legal costs ([2Keller](https://www.2keller.com/library/have-you-had-a-slip-and-fall-in-a-grocery-store-in-indiana.cfm));
  - about $50,000 average defence cost per claim ([CleanLink](https://www.cleanlink.com/cleanlinkminute/details/The-Cost-Of-Slips-Trips-and-Falls--46546));
  - about 60% of grocery general-liability claims ([Insuranceopedia](https://www.insuranceopedia.com/business-insurance/grocery-store-insurance-cost)).

  Separately, Liberty Mutual's 2024 index ranks falls on the same level as the second-costliest workplace injury cause, at about $9.99B a year ([Evotix summary](https://evotix.com/resources/blog/the-1-billion-hidden-cost-of-poor-ehs-management)).
- **Why the timing of a spill matters.** In slip-and-fall cases the key question is how long the hazard was there and when the store last inspected. Texas requires evidence of time ([JD Supra on the Texas Supreme Court](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/supreme-court-of-texas-reaffirms-strict-3738536/)). Sweep logs are evidence that can help or hurt either side ([Marshall Dennehey](https://marshalldennehey.com/articles/winning-safety-sweep-stakes-impact-inspection-protocals-retailer-slip-and-fall-litigation)). **A timestamped "seen, then fixed" record is exactly that kind of evidence.**
- **Blocked exits and aisles.** Dollar General agreed to pay $12M to OSHA over blocked exits, electrical panels and fire extinguishers, plus unsafe storage. It must now fix such hazards within 48 hours or face up to $100,000 a day ([News4Jax](https://news4jax.com/news/local/2024/07/12/dollar-general-agrees-to-pay-12-million-fine-improve-safety-conditions-for-store-workers), [NBC](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/business/money-report/dollar-general-settles-with-the-labor-department-over-workplace-safety-violations/3424308/)).

## 3. Regulation and trust

- **Face recognition carries real legal risk in retail.**
  - The FTC banned Rite Aid from using face recognition for 5 years after thousands of false matches, with more false matches in Black and Asian neighbourhoods ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/19/ftc-bars-rite-aid-from-using-facial-recognition-under-settlement.html)).
  - Illinois BIPA damages are now one recovery per person, but still $1,000 for negligent and $5,000 for reckless violations (SB 2979, Aug 2024, [Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/illinois-legislature-amends-bipa-to-limit-damages-and-expand-consent-options/)). A shopper sued Walmart over Clearview face matching ([NBC Chicago](https://nbcchicago.com/news/local/class-action-complaint-filed-against-walmart-under-same-law-behind-650-million-facebook-settlement/2953179)).
  - New York City's biometric law covers retail and food-and-drink businesses. It requires signs at entrances, bans selling the data, and sets $500 to $5,000 per violation. Ordinary video is generally exempt from the sign rule ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2021/05/05_19-nyc-enacts-biometric-data-disclosure-rules)). It has been tested against Amazon Go ([JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/plaintiffs-attorneys-continue-to-test-2215125)).
  - **Rulecam already blurs faces and never recognises them** (YuNet blur in `runtime/strip_draw.py`, per `WHAT-WE-BUILT.md`). That is a selling point, not just a footnote.
- **EU AI Act.** Since 2 Feb 2025 it bans emotion recognition at work, the untargeted scraping of faces from CCTV, and biometric categorisation of sensitive traits ([Mishcon de Reya](https://www.mishcon.com/news/eu-ai-act-begins-to-take-effect-what-to-know-and-how-to-prepare), [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/global/ai-and-the-workplace-navigating-prohibited-ai-practices-in-the-eu)). "Staff friendliness" scoring of the Burger King kind would be in danger in the EU.
- **GDPR and CCTV.**
  - The EDPB's video guidelines call for layered signs and storage limitation ([EDPB 3/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en.pdf)). Commentary on the draft suggested short retention of a few days (unverified against the final text, [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/edpb-adopts-guidelines-on-data-processing-through-video-devices)).
  - The UK ICO's 2023 guidance on monitoring workers says monitoring must be necessary and proportionate, workers must be told, and a DPIA is needed where special-category data is involved ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/data-privacy/uk-information-commissioners-office-releases-rew-guidance-for-monitoring-at-work/)).
  - **Our station-level scorecards (`chain/scorecard.py`, which never groups by person) and visible rules fit this well.**
- **Food safety.**
  - Handwashing was the most common Food Code violation in an analysis of about 2 million inspections ([The Takeout](https://thetakeout.com/restaurant-food-safety-violation-fda-hand-washing-rules-1850972430), [NRN](https://nrn.com/operations/food-health-and-safety-compliance-not-vigilant-post-pandemic-world)). The FDA's own risk-factor study agrees ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/194812/download)).
  - Food Code 5-205.11 says the hand sink must be accessible at all times and used only for handwashing. Florida treats a breach as a Priority Foundation violation ([Florida food safety](https://floridafoodsafety.org/fdacs-violation/5-20511-handwashing-sink-not-maintained-so-that), [Minnesota rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/minnesota/Minn-R-4626-1110)).
  - **A camera can check a blocked hand sink well. It cannot check water temperature, and the planner should say so.**
- **Worker and union concerns** are mostly about scoring individuals ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/burger-king-ai-assistant-patty-headsets-restaurants/), [CCW](https://customercontactweekdigital.com/tools-technologies/news/outback-steakhouse-nixes-employee-surveillance-program-following-customer-backlash)). We found no specific union statement on grocery cameras (unverified).
- **Insurance and liability.** See the slip-and-fall and OSHA figures in section 2. We found no insurer that publicly discounts premiums for AI camera evidence (unverified, so don't claim it).

## 4. Voids, ranked

Ranked by how often the problem happens, what it costs a small shop, how badly current products cover it, and how well Rulecam fits.

1. **"Seen, then fixed" proof for floor and exit hazards (spills, boxes in aisles, blocked exits and extinguishers).**
   - The cost is big, and the evidence is about time: slip-and-fall cases turn on how long a spill was there, and Dollar General was fined $12M for blocked exits.
   - Spill detection exists only for enterprises (Dragonfruit, icetana) and has a false-alarm problem.
   - **Nobody sells a verified alert plus a tamper-evident record of when it was fixed.**
   - Rulecam fit: Glimmer verification, the evidence chain, incident packs and WhatsApp acknowledgements already exist.
2. **Shelf availability for small stores without new hardware.**
   - Out-of-stocks cost about 7% of sales (NielsenIQ, cited above).
   - Focal, Simbe and Trax are built for chains and brands and need new cameras, robots or data integrations.
   - Rulecam's `shelf/gaps.py` already uses two models that check each other (Spark finds the gap, SAM confirms it is empty). What's missing is the loop: scan on a schedule, alert, confirm the refill, and record how long it took.
3. **Answers with numbers and a receipt, from the cameras a shop already has.**
   - Owners don't watch footage (under 1% is viewed).
   - Plain-language search is now cheap at home ($20 a month from Google and Ring) but has no counts, rules or proof.
   - Business plain-language search needs vendor cameras (Verkada) or enterprise contracts (Conntour, Hakimo).
   - Rulecam's Ask already does SQL over tracks, SAM counts, Glimmer looking at frames and Spark watching video, with a hash-chained run log. What's missing is a multilingual voice front end and a verifiable answer card.
4. **Food-safety self-checks that score the station, not the cook.**
   - Handwashing is the most common violation.
   - Products in this space (Wobot, Agot, Presto) watch people, and that causes backlash.
   - Rulecam's `advisor/kitchen.py`, the `food_code` pack in `evidence/packs.py`, and `chain/scorecard.py` (which never groups by person) cover this. A supermarket deli or bakery counter is the same problem.
5. **Queue alerts spoken in the staff's own language.**
   - Shoppers leave at a line of three or four.
   - Enterprise analytics count queues but don't tell the stocker in Spanish to open till 2.
   - Rulecam's `crowd/warden.py` already has zones, Spark assessment, a Glimmer check and MMS-TTS announcements.
6. **Theft alerts verified without faces.**
   - This is real, but it is crowded and cheap: Veesion is about $200 a month and works.
   - Our edge is a second model checking each alert before a lone cashier is pulled away, and an evidence pack without biometrics.
   - Not worth new build time before the deadline.

## 5. Open-source and public assets we could reuse now

- **Roboflow `supervision`** (MIT, already a dependency at 0.30.0). Has a queue tutorial using PolygonZone and time-in-zone dwell per person, written to CSV ([Roboflow](https://blog.roboflow.com/monitor-retail-queues/)). Use it as the reference for queue dwell in feature 4.
- **Frigate** (MIT). Generates AI descriptions of events and supports semantic search (CLIP or MiniLM, stored in ChromaDB) ([Frigate GenAI docs](https://docs.frigate.video/category/generative-ai), [semantic search](https://deploy-preview-13991--frigate-docs.netlify.app/configuration/semantic_search)). A good design reference for Ask's caption memory, and evidence that small shops expect "search by words".
- **go2rtc** (MIT). Turns RTSP from any existing NVR into WebRTC ([pkg.go.dev](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/AlexxIT/go2rtc)). It is the quickest way to make the claim "works on the cameras you already have" true for a shop's DVR.
- **SKU-110K.** 11,743 photos of densely packed shelves, with one class, "product" ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/datasets/detect/sku-110k), [GitHub](https://github.com/eg4000/SKU110K_CVPR19)). Its licence is reported as academic and non-commercial (Trax), unverified. We could use a few images as shelf-gap test stills for Rule CI and a scored eval of `shelf/gaps.py`.
- **PoseLift** (WACV 2025). A real shoplifting dataset released as pose data only, to protect privacy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06591v1)). **RetailS** (Mar 2026) is a multi-camera retail shoplifting dataset ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04723v1)). Licence terms not checked.
- **Stock footage.** Coverr has free supermarket and grocery clips ([Coverr](https://coverr.co/stock-video-footage/supermarket)). The Pexels licence allows free commercial use without attribution, according to secondary sources; check [pexels.com/license](https://www.pexels.com/license/) before use. Branded packs and identifiable people in clips need care.
- **Already in Rulecam and worth naming in the demo:**
  - SAM 3.1 for counting and confirming;
  - Muse Glimmer on our GPU for verification;
  - Voice Transcribe in `clients/meta_hosted/voice.py`;
  - MMS-TTS. Its licence is CC-BY-NC, so it is fine for the demo but not for a commercial release.

## 6. Five feature specs for the supermarket demo

Ranked by judge impact divided by effort. Every one reuses existing modules. None needs new hardware, and none needs faces.

### F1. Shelf gap to restock loop, with "refilled in N minutes" (2.5 h)

- **Problem and who pays.** An empty facing loses sales until someone notices (about 7% of sales by NielsenIQ's count). The store owner or category manager pays. For brands it is a merchandising-compliance question; Trax charges for that at enterprise scale.
- **What the user sees.**
  1. The owner says: "Check the pasta aisle every 10 minutes and tell Maria what to bring."
  2. Maria gets a WhatsApp message with the marked shelf photo: "Bay 3, 4 facings empty, next to the Barilla and Rao's packs." Her buttons are *On my way* and *False alarm*.
  3. The next scan sees the gap filled and closes it: "Refilled in 7 min."
  4. The dashboard shows today's gaps and how long each took to fill.
- **Meta models.**
  - Muse Spark 1.3 finds gaps and reads the neighbouring packs, as it already does.
  - SAM 3.1 confirms no product is in the gap, as it already does.
  - Muse Glimmer gives a yes/no on the marked crop before anyone is messaged. This is new, so that "Glimmer verifies every alert" holds here too.
  - WhatsApp Cloud API with reply buttons.
- **What it extends.**
  - `src/rulecam/shelf/gaps.py` `ShelfScanner.scan_camera`: add a `ShelfWatch` ticker on the `ask/memory.py` `CaptionMemory.tick` worker-thread pattern.
  - Match gaps across scans by IoU on `ShelfGap.box_xyxy` to open and close a "gap episode".
  - Send through `act/escalation.py` and `act/whatsapp.py`, with the marked image as the header.
  - Write episodes to a hash-chained JSONL like `ask/runlog.py` does. Use a separate log because the evidence chain's record kinds are fixed by the contracts.
  - Expose a `shelf_gaps` table in the `ask/data.py` snapshot so Ask can answer "which shelf was empty longest today?".
- **Effort.**
  - Ticker and episode matching: 1 h.
  - Glimmer check and WhatsApp: 0.5 h.
  - Hash-chained log and Ask table: 0.5 h.
  - UI list: 0.5 h.
- **Demo moment.** Pull six jars off a real shelf (or a pantry shelf) in front of the phone camera. The WhatsApp arrives with the numbered gap. Put the jars back. Show "Refilled in 2 min 40 s" with its receipt hash, then ask Ask how long the shelf was empty today.

### F2. Ask by voice in any language, with an answer card you can verify (2 h)

- **Problem and who pays.** Owners never review footage (under 1% is viewed) and finding the right clip takes too long. Owners and managers pay, and so do their insurers and lawyers when a claim arrives. Home tools now answer plain-language questions for $20 a month, but with no counts and no proof.
- **What the user sees.**
  1. The owner holds the mic and asks in Spanish or Hindi: "When did the milk fridge first look empty today, and how many people were queuing at 5 pm?"
  2. The answer comes back spoken in the same language, with timestamps, a SAM count, a still image, and the receipt.
  3. "Download answer card" gives a one-page PDF with the question, the answer, the steps the models took, and the receipt hash. A QR code opens a browser page that recomputes the hash.
  4. Supermarket question chips appear when the site profile is `supermarket`.
- **Meta models.**
  - Voice Transcribe (with languageBias) turns speech into the question.
  - Muse Spark 1.3 plans the steps and writes the answer.
  - SAM 3.1 does the counting.
  - Glimmer looks at the frames.
  - Spark watches the video.
  - MMS-TTS speaks the answer.
- **What it extends.**
  - `src/rulecam/ask/agent.py` (`AskAgent`), `ask/runlog.py` (the `sha256` receipt), `clients/meta_hosted/voice.py`, `voice_out/tts.py`, and `context/site_context.py` (the `supermarket` vertical).
  - The answer card reuses `evidence/packs.py` (`render_pdf`, `qr_data_uri`).
  - The verify page reuses the canonical JSON from `evidence/chain.py`.
  - UI: `ui/src/app/ask`.
- **Effort.**
  - Voice endpoint and spoken answer: 0.75 h.
  - Answer-card PDF and QR: 0.75 h.
  - Chips and UI: 0.5 h.
- **Demo moment.** A teammate asks in Spanish. The answer is spoken back in Spanish with a count and a still. A judge scans the QR on the card and their own browser recomputes the receipt.

### F3. Hazard watch for spills and blocked exits, with a seen-to-cleared record (3 h)

- **Problem and who pays.**
  - Slip-and-fall claims are about 60% of grocery liability claims (attributed to NFSI, unverified) and turn on how long the hazard was there.
  - Blocked exits cost Dollar General $12M.
  - The owner and their insurer pay. A record that a hazard was "seen at 14:02:10, acknowledged 14:03:05, cleared 14:05:41" is the evidence that wins or loses these cases.
- **What the user sees.**
  - The owner says: "Tell me about spills or broken jars in the aisles, and anything blocking the fire exit for more than 5 minutes."
  - The planner says what it cannot check honestly: clear water on a shiny floor is hard to see, so the alert is verified by a second model rather than trusted from one frame.
  - Alerts go out by WhatsApp. When the hazard is cleared, a one-click incident PDF shows seen, acknowledged and cleared times, blurred stills, and a QR code to the verify page.
- **Meta models.**
  - Muse Spark 1.3 proposes hazard boxes on each frame, following the `shelf/gaps.py` pattern.
  - SAM 3.1 checks the solid ones it can segment: "cardboard box", "pallet" or "cart" inside the exit-zone polygon. We have **not** tested SAM on puddles; if it fails, a spill alert needs Spark and Glimmer to agree.
  - Muse Glimmer gives a yes/no on the frame strip before any alert.
  - The blocked exit becomes a **typed rule through the existing planner**: object in the exit zone, held for 5 minutes, using `rules/temporal.py`. Rule CI then attacks the rule (boundary at 4:59 and 5:01) before it goes live.
- **What it extends.**
  - New `src/rulecam/shelf/hazards.py`, a sibling of `gaps.py` with the same two-model check.
  - Planner (`planner/steps/compile.py` already has prompts for buffers around fixed things such as spills).
  - `rules/temporal.py`, `verify/tier1.py`, `act/escalation.py`, and `evidence/incident.py` (`build_incident`, `report_document`).
- **Effort.**
  - `hazards.py` scan and episode: 1.25 h.
  - Exit rule through the planner and Rule CI: 0.5 h.
  - Incident PDF with cleared time: 0.75 h.
  - Rehearsal: 0.5 h.
- **Demo moment.** Pour a cup of water on the floor and drop a box in front of the "fire exit" (a taped door frame). Glimmer confirms the box and rejects a shadow. Someone taps *On my way*, wipes the floor and moves the box. The incident PDF shows "cleared in 3 min 31 s" and the browser verifies the chain.

### F4. Queue to "open till 2", spoken in the staff's language (2 h)

- **Problem and who pays.** About half of convenience-store shoppers would leave at a four-person line (vendor survey). The store manager pays in lost baskets.
- **What the user sees.**
  1. The owner draws the queue zone and says: "If more than 3 people wait at till 1 for over a minute, ask for till 2, in English and Spanish."
  2. When that happens, the phone speaker plays the announcement, staff get a WhatsApp, and the record shows the queue length and wait when till 2 opened.
  3. A what-if line uses measured arrivals: "a second till would cut the average wait from about X to about Y", computed with Erlang C, not guessed by a model.
- **Meta models.**
  - Muse Spark 1.3 assesses the queue against the owner's sentence, as the crowd warden already does.
  - SAM 3.1 counts people in the zone.
  - Glimmer checks the frame before the alert.
  - MMS-TTS speaks the announcement.
- **What it extends.**
  - `src/rulecam/crowd/warden.py` (`CrowdWarden.watch` with a supermarket instruction) and `crowd/zones.py` (`CrowdZone`).
  - `advisor/whatif/queueing.py` (Little, Erlang C) for the what-if line.
  - `act/whatsapp.py`.
  - Optionally, `supervision` time-in-zone for per-person waiting time.
- **Effort.** Config and instruction: 0.5 h. Queue count and dwell: 0.75 h. What-if line: 0.5 h. UI text: 0.25 h.
- **Demo moment.** Four teammates line up. Glimmer confirms "4 waiting, one till open". The laptop says "Abran la caja dos, por favor." The what-if line appears with its numbers.

### F5. Deli and bakery food-safety self-inspection pack, station-level only (2 h)

- **Problem and who pays.** Handwashing is the most common Food Code violation, and a blocked hand sink is a Priority Foundation citation in Florida. The store owner, the deli manager and the chain's head office pay. Today's tools (Wobot, Agot) watch individual workers and draw backlash.
- **What the user sees.**
  - A "Supermarket deli basics" rule pack. Its slots are in words:
    - "the deli hand sink";
    - "the ready-to-eat case";
    - "the raw meat prep board".
  - Its rules:
    - the hand sink is never blocked for more than 2 minutes (5-205.11);
    - handwashes are counted against the counter's busy periods, at station level;
    - raw meat trays never go on the ready-to-eat shelf.
  - The inspector-ready PDF covers each clause, its rule, the compliance rate with a 95% interval, BLIND time kept out, skeleton thumbnails, and a QR code. There are no names and no per-person numbers.
- **Meta models.**
  - Muse Spark plans the pack and says what a camera cannot check (water temperature, soap).
  - SAM 3.1 handles the zones and objects (box at the sink, meat tray).
  - Glimmer verifies each alert.
  - Spark Open audits the plan if its key is available. It is wired but not live.
- **What it extends.**
  - `chains/kitchen-basics`: copy it to `chains/supermarket-deli`.
  - `chain/pack.py` (`prove_pack`), `chain/fit.py`, `chain/scorecard.py`.
  - `advisor/kitchen.py` (hand-sink blocked minutes, handwash against load, sanitising cadence).
  - `evidence/packs.py` (the `food_code` template).
- **Effort.** Pack YAML and slots: 0.75 h. Rule CI green at the knob ends: 0.5 h. Pack PDF and wording: 0.75 h.
- **Demo moment.** Put a crate in front of the sink. An alert fires and the crate is removed. The PDF reads: "Hand sink blocked 2 min 10 s today, fixed; watched 96% of open hours; no person is identified." Then `uv run rulecam chain demo chains/supermarket-deli` shows the same pack proved for a 10-store chain.

## 7. Positioning lines (never "first natural-language camera rules")

1. **vs Verkada, Rhombus and Spot AI:** Keep the cameras you have, and your phone. Rulecam adds checking, not another licence that can switch your cameras off.
2. **vs Veesion and Everseen:** Not just theft. Spills, empty shelves, queues and the hand sink too, and a second model checks every alert before your only cashier leaves the till.
3. **vs Brivo Eeva, Coram and Lumana:** Others let you type an alert. Rulecam tells you what the camera can't check, attacks the rule before it goes live, and keeps a record anyone can verify afterwards.
4. **vs Google and Ring "ask your camera":** Home cameras tell you what happened. Rulecam answers with counts, timestamps and a receipt good enough for an inspector or an insurer.
5. **vs Wobot, Agot and Presto:** It scores the hand sink, not the cook. No faces, no friendliness scores, no per-worker numbers.

**Best line:** *Your cameras already see the spill, the empty shelf and the queue. Rulecam tells the right person in their own language, checks it twice, and keeps a receipt that it was fixed.*
