# Rulecam on smart glasses: live results, Nov 4 2026

Branch `p1/glasses`. Every number below comes from a live call to the real models, recorded in
`reports/eval/2026-11-04-glasses-a.json` and `-b.json` by `scripts/eval/glasses_live.py`. Nothing
here was run on real glasses. See `docs/usecases/glasses.md` for how it works.

## In short

- The whole path works live, end to end: a frame and a spoken question go in, Muse Voice
  Transcribe hears it, Muse Glimmer looks, SAM 3.1 counts, Muse Spark 1.3 checks and answers, and
  Meta MMS-TTS speaks it. A safety walk ends in a Spark report whose hazards are in the evidence
  chain (`verify_chain`: ok, 8 records), in `GET /incidents` (with the "confirmed" verdict where two
  models verified them live) and in the one-click incident report
  (`GET /incidents/{id}/report`, "Safety walk with smart glasses").
- Two-model **verified** alerts happened live: the forklift-aisle question in run B and two walk
  frames in run A were flagged by Glimmer and confirmed by Spark, with SAM 3.1 counting the
  forklift and the person.
- **It is slow for an earpiece.** A question takes a median of 28 s (run A) to 33 s (run B) from
  frame to spoken WAV. A quiet patrol frame costs one Glimmer look, 8 s. The time goes to Glimmer's
  look (median 14.5 s in run B), Spark's answer (median 11 s) and SAM 3.1 (4 s).
- The models disagree with each other often, and the cross-check catches it: SAM 3.1 found no
  forklift where Glimmer saw one far ahead, and Spark refused to report shelf gaps it could see were
  stocked.

## What we learned

1. **Cross-checking works and is needed.** On the walk's first frame (a worker walking down the
   aisle), Glimmer reported a forklift far ahead; SAM 3.1 counted no forklift and Spark declined to
   raise it. In an earlier test on another bread-aisle frame, the shelf check confirmed three
   "gaps" (SAM 3.1 found no "product package" in boxes that held bagged bread), and Spark, looking
   at the numbered frame, said the shelves were stocked. In both runs here the shelf check found no
   gaps on the two aisle frames, and the answers said so.
2. **SAM 3.1 can be confidently wrong too.** On the forklift-beside-the-camera frame it boxed the
   forklift's red warning light as a "hard hat", and Spark repeated it ("wearing a red hard hat").
   Glimmer named the concept; nothing checks a SAM detection against the frame except Spark.
3. **The same frame does not always get the same verdict.** The two forklift walk frames were
   verified alerts in run A, and in run B one was judged "caution, not an alert" by both models
   and the other was never seen by Glimmer (it timed out at 25 s), so it was not verified and not
   spoken. Only verified patrol alerts are spoken, by design; a single model's alert is shown, not
   spoken.
4. **Latency is the main gap.** 30 s from question to answer is too slow for an earpiece. Glimmer
   on one RTX 4090 at about 34 tokens a second (the Nov 2 budget sweep) is the largest part; Spark's
   answer is the next. Glimmer's thinking budget does not help (that sweep: 6.4 s median with no
   budget and with any budget from 128 to 1,024 tokens). What would: shorter model outputs (now
   asked for, not yet measured), self-hosted SAM 3.1 on the GPU pod, more Glimmer workers, and
   speaking Glimmer's first look while Spark checks it.
5. **Cold start.** The serverless Glimmer had no workers; the first requests waited about two
   minutes for one. The page now pings Glimmer when it opens and every minute while open, and while
   Glimmer is not known to be up, a question is looked at by Glimmer and Spark at once.
6. **Voice Transcribe** heard "shelf" as "shell" (run A) and doubled a word twice ("safe to safe
   to", "howhow"). The models still answered the intended question. Run B biased transcription
   toward each mode's words; "shelf" was then heard right.
7. **Privacy wording is only partly followed.** The prompts say clothing may be mentioned only as
   safety equipment. Spark's on-screen detail still described clothing colours ("the cashier in
   yellow", "a white shirt and red headwear"). The spoken lines did not. Nobody was identified.
8. **Hosted Glimmer elsewhere is no faster.** Routeway's hosted `muse-glimmer-30b` refused for lack
   of credit; its free tier answered in 37 s once, and in another call its reply was cut at 1,024
   tokens and retried, past a 90 s timeout. Not used in these runs.

## The phone page, checked in a browser

At 01:20 UTC, on the final code with Glimmer up, the `/glasses` page (375 by 812, the desktop app's
browser pane, local API) answered "Is it safe to walk ahead?" on the forklift-aisle photo with a
**verified alert** in 29.9 s (look 16.8, count 2.4, compose 7.7, speak 3.0), showing each stage
in the HUD as it finished, the red caption, SAM 3.1's boxes and the Meta MMS-TTS voice label. A
safety walk started from the page sent two patrol frames (both verified alerts) and finished into a
report with two caution hazards, "chained as evidence: 3 records". Screens are in
`apps/glasses/screens/`.

That session found one bug, fixed after it: a patrol frame still in flight when the wearer tapped
**Finish walk** came back as an error ("walk is finished") instead of an answer. The answer is now
kept (not added to the finished walk), and the page no longer counts it on the walk.

## Changed after the runs (not measured)

- The prompts now forbid describing clothing colours, hair, age or other looks (point 7).
- Model outputs are asked to be shorter (point 4). This was already in the browser check above
  (one answer, compose 7.7 s), but not in runs A and B; one answer is not a measurement.
- A Glimmer that is up stays "up" while it is pinged again; before, each minute's ping briefly
  marked it "checking", which made questions race Spark needlessly.

## Not done or not verified

- No real glasses, no phone: frames were stock video stills and questions were synthetic speech.
- The browser camera and push-to-talk recording were not exercised (the browser pane blocks
  them); the WAV path behind them was.
- Answers playing through Bluetooth glasses: not tried.
- The native app on Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit: planned in `apps/glasses/README.md`,
  not built.
- Latency improvements in point 4 are not measured.
- No ground truth was labelled; the right answers above were judged by eye from the frames.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv sync --group dev --extra ml          # MMS-TTS for the server voice
make api                                 # with META_API_KEY and the Glimmer settings
uv run python scripts/eval/glasses_live.py --api http://127.0.0.1:8000 \
  --frames data/wearables-eval --out reports/eval/glasses-run.json --label mine
```

`data/wearables-eval` held stills cut from `data/testclips` with `ffmpeg -ss <s> -frames:v 1
-vf scale=960:-2` (forklift aisle `pexels-4294434` at 1 s and 9 s, forklift `pexels-32243653` at
2 s, bread aisle `market/p39238831` at 0 s and 8 s, checkouts `market2/p39221981` and
`market2/p36635591` at 2 s) and three questions made with `say -o q.wav
--data-format=LEI16@16000 "<question>"`.
